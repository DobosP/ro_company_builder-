# Greenhouse Ecosystem — indoor duckweed pond, exotic crops, (and cows?)

> Refining the [small-start plan](phase1-small-start.md): put the **duckweed pond inside the greenhouse**, build a **warm-humid tropical microclimate** for **year-round exotic/rare crops & condiments**, and weigh **adding cattle**. Fish dropped. Verified 2026-05; [I#] → [sources](research/sources.md).

## 1. Duckweed pond *inside* the greenhouse? ✅ Yes — and it's a triple win
Putting the pond indoors is actually **better** than outdoors here:
- **Year-round duckweed:** outdoors it goes dormant/dies in a RO winter; **inside the heated greenhouse it grows all year** → continuous fertiliser (and feed). [I11](research/sources.md#i11)
- **Solves part of your heating problem:** water is excellent **thermal mass** — the pond soaks up day heat and releases it overnight, exactly the passive-heating trick you need (like the water barrels, but bigger). [I13](research/sources.md#i13)
- **Free humidity:** evaporation keeps the air humid — perfect for **tropical exotics** (ginger, turmeric, vanilla) that demand "constant humidity." [I17](research/sources.md#i17)

**Design tips (so it doesn't cost you growing space or cause disease):**
- Build it as **shallow channels / raised tanks along the north wall** (dead growing zone anyway) — doubles as the insulated thermal-mass wall.
- **Duckweed cover suppresses mosquitoes & algae** by shading the water — keep the surface fully covered; skim & harvest regularly.
- **Feed the pond nutrients** (diluted manure / compost tea) — duckweed needs N/P to grow fast; this is where any livestock manure plugs in.
- **Manage humidity** with automatic roof vents — too humid → fungal disease on non-tropical crops. So **zone it**: pond + tropicals in the humid end; herbs/greens in a drier, ventilated end.

## 2. Year-round exotic / rare crops — yes, this is the high-margin core
A heated, humid greenhouse lets you grow **import-substitution exotics** that Romania buys 100% imported — premium price, small footprint. Ranked for **value vs effort** (start low-effort):

| Crop | Value | Effort | Fit with the pond microclimate |
|------|-------|--------|-------------------------------|
| **Ginger & turmeric** ⭐ | high (superfood) | **low** | loves 22–25°C + humidity — *ideal* [I17](research/sources.md#i17) |
| **Exotic herbs** (Thai/holy basil, lemongrass, kaffir lime, shiso, vanilla grass) | high €/m² | **low**, fast cycles | humidity-friendly [I15](research/sources.md#i15) |
| **Specialty chillies** (habanero, exotic capsicum) | high | low–med | warm, perennial indoors |
| **Microgreens** | very high €/m² | low–med | fast cash between crops [I15](research/sources.md#i15) |
| **Cardamom / black pepper / vanilla** | very high | med–high (vanilla = hand-pollinated, 3–4 yr) | tropical, humidity-loving — later add-ons |
| **Saffron** | highest €/kg | **high** (hand-harvest stigmas) | a laborious premium line — add once you have help |

**Recommendation:** anchor on **ginger + turmeric + exotic herbs + specialty chillies** (low effort, year-round, premium, thrive in the pond's warm-humid air). Add **vanilla/saffron/cardamom** later as high-value "trophy" lines when you have seasonal help. This *is* the "ecosystem in a greenhouse" — a tropical microclimate selling rare condiments all winter while Romania imports them.

## 3. Cows fed on duckweed? Honest answer: **not yet**
- **Can duckweed feed cattle?** Yes — **as a supplement, not the diet.** It's up to **43% crude protein**, nutritionally **between grass silage and soybean meal** [I16](research/sources.md#i16). But it works only at **~20% of the ration** (full substitution lowers performance), and **cows eat huge volumes** (~12–18 kg dry matter/day each). Your in-greenhouse pond can't grow nearly enough to matter for cattle.
- **Cows break your "low-effort, solo" goal:** they add a **barn, daily care, vet, ANSVSA registration, animal-welfare rules, manure management, and a lot of forage** — the heaviest livestock option. That contradicts "manageable with less effort."
- **The elegant loop does exist** — manure → duckweed pond → protein feed → animals — but it fits **poultry or a couple of goats** far better than cows (lighter, and the duckweed-feed evidence is strongest for poultry/small ruminants). [I16](research/sources.md#i16)
- **Verdict:** **defer cattle.** Keep Phase 1 = greenhouse + indoor duckweed pond + exotic crops (low effort, grant-funded, profitable). If you want animals later for the manure→duckweed loop, **start with chickens or 2–3 goats**, not cows.
